\paragraph{A live contradiction about generation length.}
Whether low-bit quantization lengthens chain-of-thought output is currently
disputed. \citet{lian2026inflates} report that INT4/INT3 quantization can
preserve accuracy while \emph{increasing} reasoning-token usage, and propose a
CoT Token Inflation Ratio to quantify it; \citet{lotfi2026longer} likewise find
that aggressive post-training quantization lengthens CoT. \citet{liu2025quantization}
report the opposite: ``contrary to expectations, quantized models do not exhibit
increased output lengths.'' We take no position on which generator behaviour is
correct, because the disagreement has a consequence that neither side measures.
Under a \emph{fixed} generation cap, a model that emits longer traces is scored
wrong more often whenever its answer falls beyond the cap --- a scoring outcome,
not a competence one. No study on either side of this dispute sweeps the
generation cap, so no study can separate the two. That separation is our H1.

\paragraph{Degradation has already been partly explained --- by other causes.}
\citet{kurtic2025bf16} show that reported quantization degradation was
overstated, and attribute the excess to untuned quantization-side
hyperparameters. We treat that result as established and \emph{prior}, not as a
target: our claim concerns the residual that remains after quantization-side
tuning, and our mechanism is orthogonal to theirs. \citet{lotfi2026longer}
supply the most direct antecedent for the present work, finding that in as many
as 52\% of quantized-model failures the model reaches the correct answer in an
intermediate step but does not emit it as the final answer. Their interest is in
fixing the generator, via a logit penalty on overthinking markers; ours is in
what such failures do to a reported accuracy delta when they are left in place.

\paragraph{The centre of gravity runs the other way.}
The dominant finding in this literature is that benchmarks \emph{understate}
quantization damage, not overstate it. \citet{marchisio2024multilingual} find a
1.7\% automatic drop in Japanese corresponds to 16.0\% by human judgement, and
\citet{dutta2024accuracy} show that models matched on accuracy still diverge
behaviourally --- the ``flips'' framing, which is theirs and which we do not
claim. We regard both directions as simultaneously true: an accuracy delta can
over-penalise formatting \emph{and} under-detect behavioural change, because
these are different quantities. Our contribution is to the first, and it does
not license any conclusion about the second.

\paragraph{Calibrating extraction, not loosening it.}
An argument that evaluation should simply be more permissive is already
refuted. \citet{ren2026shortcut} find that 8.2--44.1\% of answers graded correct
are reached by shortcut hacking, so lenient extraction over-credits as surely as
strict extraction under-credits. We therefore treat the strict/permissive
contrast as a measurement instrument to be characterised rather than a knob to
be turned in one direction, and we report both rungs throughout.

\paragraph{Prompt- and protocol-sensitivity.}
\citet{hua2025flaw} ask whether prompt sensitivity is a genuine model property
or an artifact of evaluation, and we are structurally the sequel: they vary the
prompt, we vary the scorer, and both ask whether a reported effect survives the
instrument. Their null result on MATH deserves emphasis rather than a footnote
--- extraction there already uses symbolic equivalence, so a well-engineered
verifier leaves little for a protocol effect to explain. This bounds our claim
rather than supporting it, and it is why our task selection targets benchmarks
whose canonical extractors are regular expressions.
\citet{sclar2024formatspread} establish that spurious formatting choices move
scores substantially, and \citet{jo2025finding} and \citet{yu2025xfinder} show
that answer extraction from reasoning traces is itself an unreliable step.
\citet{schaeffer2023mirage} provide the methodological template we follow: a
headline effect reattributed, in part, to the measurement instrument.
The lm-evaluation-harness \citep{biderman2024lessons} is our instrument, and its
canonical filters are the object of study rather than an implementation detail.

\paragraph{Serving configuration is a confound we pin, not an axis we explore.}
\citet{pape2026silent} find that inference-backend choice alone moves benchmark
scores by up to 16.6 points, and \citet{masoudian2026sideeffect} attribute
roughly 39\% of out-of-the-box variance to it. We read this as a reason to
freeze the engine, version, and every decoding parameter across all arms, which
we do and report; a floating serving configuration would make precision effects
unidentifiable. We claim no novelty on this axis.

\paragraph{Adjacent evaluations.}
Broad compression benchmarks \citep{vonrad2026unicomp,wang2025slmquant} and
task-difficulty studies \citep{lee2025tradeoffs} establish the accuracy
landscape we work inside; \citet{lee2025tradeoffs} in particular report that
quantization disrupts instruction-following, which is the strongest available
objection to our framing and which our compliance/competence split is designed
to address directly rather than deflect. \citet{prasad2026factorial} run a
factorial quantization analysis, so factorial design is not our contribution;
their outcome domain is safety alignment under sampling temperature, which is
disjoint from ours. \citet{kurt2026which} report the observation this paper
sets out to explain --- strict-versus-flexible GSM8K gaps of 41 to 64 points
across fourteen \texttt{llama.cpp} quantizations --- without interpreting the
gap; we reproduce their numbers as an external anchor.
\citet{oladri2026silent} and \citet{rasmussen2026calibration} report related
failure-mode and scoring-rule effects, the former over FP32/FP16/NF4 only and
so without bearing on GPTQ or AWQ.

\paragraph{What is new here.}
Permissive extraction is well known to raise absolute scores. What has not been
established is whether it changes the quantization \emph{difference}: an
artifact in a level need not be an artifact in a delta, and the two are
routinely conflated. We decompose the measured strict delta into competence,
compliance, and truncation components, pre-register the decomposition before any
generation exists, and report the residual precision effect that survives it.
