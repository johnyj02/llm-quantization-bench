## Q1 — Move to 32. My R16.2 rationale is void, and the cost is smaller than you've framed it.

Your point (2) is the substantive one and it retires my ruling: I froze 64 on the grounds that GPTQ-4's level-dependence was noise, and the inert BBH items were flattening exactly that dependence. 5.8 → 8.2 → 11.2 monotone is not noise. **On the obligation: §9 says the freeze is "escalated before being made," not forbidden.** The trigger has been honoured — by this escalation. So the question is what the escalation decides, not whether we're bound to move.

**Two corrections to the cost:**

- The alternative is **32, not 8**. On a bandwidth-bound decode, 64→32 is plausibly 1.2–1.5×, not 4×. Measure it, but don't price the decision at 8's cost.
- **pilot-v2 does not need redoing.** R11/O11 already established the annotation pool needn't be byte-identical to main-v1 — judge validation is a claim about the population of generations, not about specific strings. Record that it ran at B=64 and move on. The 10-ordering H8 run is *about* the frontier and already spans 8/32/64. So the real cost is the template probe, ~$5 and one queue cycle.

**Why 32 rather than 8:** no value is clean — even B=8 sits at 5.8–7.6%. We're picking a point on a frontier, not finding safe harbour. And under fixed ordering, order-divergence contributes **zero differential noise to our deltas** (the R16.5/R17 ruling), so 8's extra 1.8 pp of margin buys defensibility on a quantity that doesn't contaminate our estimates, at 30–50 extra GPU-hours. Not worth it.

**Flag 32's fragility honestly:** 9.4% is 3 items of margin at n=500, and the trend is monotone. Say so in the paper.

**And the framing is both, not either:** report the full frontier, state that the value we originally froze exceeded our own pre-registered threshold, and that we moved. A methods paper cannot run at the setting its own pre-registration flagged — but having flagged it, moved, and reported the frontier is a *stronger* story than never having tripped the trigger.
