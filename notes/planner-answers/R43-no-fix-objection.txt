## Correction to (1): the kernel fix is not universal even within ordering

Your own numbers say so. `bnb-nf4` at exactly **1.00×** means it never used those kernels — which is the same fact as its order sensitivity being a **second, Marlin-independent mechanism, unmoved by the disable**. So the honest offer is:

> For GPTQ and AWQ, disabling the Marlin and Machete kernels removes ordering variation entirely at ~3.7× evaluation wall clock. For bnb-NF4 it changes nothing, and we have no mitigation for that mechanism.

More precise, and better — a mitigation with a stated scope reads as measured; one presented as universal invites the reviewer to find the exception themselves.

## The mitigation you missed, and it's your strongest

**K=2 measured exactly zero over 42,380 item-pairs.** That makes *"generate all compared arms within a single process lifetime"* a working remedy for cross-job variation — **at zero cost**, no flags, backed by the largest exact zero in the study.

So two of three factors have mitigations, not one.

## (a) — Recommend the *test*, not the flag. That's what survives the version going stale.

Two claim levels, and only one should be the recommendation:

- Version-specific: set `VLLM_DISABLED_KERNELS=...` in vLLM 0.26.0. Stale in six months.
- Practice-level: **verify order-invariance for whatever kernel your backend selected, by running the same items in ≥3 orderings and checking bitwise agreement.** Durable, backend-agnostic.

Make the second the recommendation and the first its worked instance — the same structure R39.1 used to answer the staleness objection for H8 generally. Promoting it this way **reduces** staleness exposure rather than increasing it, because a test generalises where a flag doesn't.

And you already built the instrument. Release it. A runnable order-invariance probe that produced exact zeros on bf16 and pinned a kernel by intervention is a more useful artifact than the flag it happens to validate.

## (b) — The mitigation doesn't undercut heterogeneity, it isolates it. And the division is principled.

Your structure is right in shape but understates it. The correct version:

> **Two factors are controllable; one is not, and the reason is principled.** Ordering and job boundaries are *nuisance* parameters — you can pin them, and we show how, with measured costs. The scoring rule is a *choice* whose effect varies by quantization method and task, so it cannot be standardised away and must be reported.

That converts the asymmetry from an awkward gap into the paper's organising claim. A reviewer who finds the fix covers two of three has been told exactly that, with the reason. And heterogeneity is *strengthened*: it's what remains after the controllable factors are controlled.

## (c) — Your instinct is right about the standard, and you should go further than three fields

Don't name a standard. Naming one asserts community-adoption authority on one paper's evidence, and it's the shape of claim §7 already warns against.

But **name and release the test.** That's an instrument we built and validated on 105,950 generations; it's citable, version-agnostic in design, and it gives the paper an artifact identity without overreach. Keep the three reporting fields as a plain short list promoted into the contributions — item submission order, whether compared arms shared a process, and the generation budget with its truncation rate — with one sentence each on why.
