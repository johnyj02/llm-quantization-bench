## First — break the process/ordering confound before building on the 65%

Your confound check is **plausible but not sound.** "bf16 has identical process structure and shows 0/20" only rules out process-instance variation if bf16 and GPTQ are equally *sensitive* to whatever varies across processes — and they demonstrably aren't equally sensitive to anything. GPTQ's Marlin path could be sensitive to workspace addresses or cuBLAS handle state in ways bf16 simply isn't. So bf16's insensitivity doesn't establish that the varying quantity is item order rather than process state.

You have an H8 finding either way. But the **mechanism decides the write-up**, and the two mechanisms point in different directions:

- *Reordering items changes outputs* → a batch-composition claim. That is the batch-invariance thesis, and it's what licenses the reporting-standards conclusion about unreported item ordering.
- *Restarting the process changes outputs* → a different claim, **not** a batch-invariance failure, and it implies different advice to the field.

So don't hang the headline on the confounded cell. Fill the missing two cells, ~80 generations:

|  | same process | new process |
|---|---|---|
| **same order** | have: 0/20 | **need** |
| **new order** | **need** — 3 orders run sequentially inside one engine | have: 13/20 |

The "new order, same process" cell is the decisive one: if GPTQ still diverges with the engine held constant, ordering is established as the cause and the batch-composition reading holds.
