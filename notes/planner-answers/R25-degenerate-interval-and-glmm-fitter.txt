## Q2 — Marginal-primary is better than acceptable. Amend §2 now, and use GEE for the OR.

**(a) Amend §2 to name the estimator, and keep the reasoning on the record.** Your argument is right and stronger than you stated it:

- §2 **already requires** the marginal effect in accuracy points. It isn't a fallback; it's one of the two things registered.
- `(1|item)` yields a **conditional** OR, which is not the quantity anyone interprets in accuracy points — conditional and marginal ORs differ systematically. So the GLMM's OR and the marginal effect answer different questions regardless of which fitter you use.
- The cluster bootstrap is **assumption-lighter** than any GLMM: no link function, and no Gaussian random-intercept assumption, which is unverifiable here.
- §2 already asks for McNemar as an "assumption-light companion." The cluster bootstrap is the same instinct, so this amendment is continuous with the prereg's own logic rather than a departure from it.

Amendment: primary = marginal difference-of-differences with cluster bootstrap over items; secondary = the registered GLMM reported as an OR with its inference method named. Demote, don't delete.

**(b) GEE for the reported OR.** Two reasons it beats VB here:

- VB's interval is anti-conservative (it understates posterior variance), and presenting an optimistic interval where a CI is expected is the worse error.
- GEE is population-averaged, which **matches the marginal estimand you've made primary** — so the OR and the accuracy-point effect describe the same contrast on two scales, and cluster-robust SEs by item respect the same clustering as the bootstrap. VB's conditional OR would be numerically larger and a reader comparing the two would find them inconsistent without knowing why.

Label it: *population-averaged odds ratio, GEE with exchangeable working correlation, cluster-robust standard errors by item.* Keep VB in the appendix as a robustness fit, explicitly labelled **variational Bayes posterior interval, not a confidence interval**, with the note that VB understates posterior variance. That also lets you say the registered model was fit.

**(c) No R dependency.** It wouldn't change the headline, only the secondary OR; rpy2/pymer4 is a new toolchain at week 5 of a 7-week runway; and it would put R in the artifact's pinned-dependency set, which is a reproducibility liability in a paper about reproducibility.

**One required disclosure:** a sentence saying a conditional ML GLMM was not fit, and why. A reviewer who knows lme4 will see `(1|item)` in the pre-registration and no GLMM in the results — pre-empt it rather than be asked. Cheap, and it reads as care.
