## But the 2.78 pp is an n=180 number, and it should not go into the prereg

The invocation effect is **churn, not bias** — your deltas run both directions within a model. Random churn means the aggregate accuracy shift scales as **1/√n**. Your floor was measured on n=180; the reference condition is n=2,719, i.e. ~15× more items, so the floor should fall by ~√15 ≈ 3.9×.

That puts the reference-n interaction floor at roughly **0.7 pp**, and ~0.5 pp after K=2 averaging — not 2.78. Baking 2.78 into §9 would cripple H3 with a number that doesn't apply to it.

**Test the scaling for free, before main-v1.** You have 300 items/cell. Compute the floor at n=45, 90, 180 and check whether it tracks 1/√n. If it does, the shrinkage prediction holds and H3 is fine. If it doesn't — if the floor is flat in n — then the effect is partly systematic rather than churn, and H3 really is in trouble. Either way you learn it today, on data in hand, before committing 17 hours.

**Prereg the estimator, not the number:** per-arm invocation SD estimated from the K=2 cells at reference n; no between-arm claim smaller than it; the n=180 pilot figure cited as a preliminary estimate with its n stated. That's honest and it doesn't pre-commit to a floor measured on the wrong sample size.

## A prereg item you haven't raised, and it's a §4.0 item

**The always-compliant stratum's membership is itself invocation-dependent.** §4.0 defines it as items where *every arm* committed — but if invocation noise flips commitment, the stratum is not a fixed set of items across invocations. That's second-order, but §4.0 is explicitly the thing that cannot be patched later.

Pre-register which: stratum defined on the **K=2-averaged** commitment, or defined as items committed in **all invocations of all arms** (stricter, smaller, invocation-stable). I'd take the latter — it's the version that can't be accused of being invocation-shopped — and report both stratum sizes.
