## Q2 — Both your candidates are right, and I'd add three more. The pattern you named is the real finding.

**Both yes:**

- **`\todo` must hit zero, and the deadline is the external-reader handoff, not the freeze.** Todos in Results exist because numbers are pending; numbers stop arriving at G7 (Sep 25). **Zero todos anywhere from Sep 30**, when the draft goes to the reader — a reader must not see them, and anything still marked by then is either a number that will never exist (cut the claim) or prose that needs writing.
- **Responsible NLP Checklist is a genuine gap**, and it's the same failure class as the Limitations grep: a desk-reject ground not covered by the gate. It needs its own instrument, since it isn't greppable from the PDF — a file with every item answered and **no blanks**. §C (compute, hyperparameters, error bars) and §E (AI assistance) apply directly.
  ⚠️ Filling §D will force you to state whether the **human annotation actually ran**. If it didn't, the judge rung is unvalidated and §3's gate was never executed — the checklist is exactly where that inconsistency surfaces, and better now than at review.

**Three more I'd add, each protecting a desk-reject ground the current four don't:**

1. **Anonymity.** Grep for author names, institution, and any GitHub/S3/bucket URL. Desk-reject ground, trivially checkable, currently unchecked.
2. **References resolve.** Assert no `?` in the PDF and that resolved `\cite` keys equal expected. CLAUDE.md records this as a past failure in this project — BibTeX drops entries silently while the PDF still builds.
3. **No load-bearing appendix content.** Not fully automatable; make it a recorded manual check at G6, per the Q1 note above.

Remove nothing. All four existing criteria are necessary.

**On the pattern, which is the more valuable thing you raised:** the gate's instrument drifting from its intent is the same failure as `desc_act` declared-but-not-forwarded, `async_scheduling` never passed, and `max_num_seqs` inherited from other hardware — one level up. The structural fix is the same one that worked there: **each criterion's check carries an inline comment naming the desk-reject ground or intent it protects.** Then the next reader can see whether the instrument still tests the intent, instead of discovering it by failing.

That's the generalisable lesson and it's worth one line in `notes/decisions.md` rather than being rediscovered a fourth time.
