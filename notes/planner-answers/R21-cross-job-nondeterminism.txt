## The clue you didn't mine: phi's split is not random

Phi's **differing** arms are bf16 and bnb-NF4. Its **stable** arms are gptq-8, gptq-4, awq. That's exactly the split between *arms that load the original bf16 weights* (bnb-NF4 quantizes in-flight from bf16) and *arms that load our S3 checkpoints* — which is also the split between **large and small memory footprint**.

That points at a specific mechanism: vLLM profiles free GPU memory at startup and derives `num_gpu_blocks` from it. Two invocations on a fresh container can measure slightly different free memory → different block count → different batch scheduling → different reduction order → one flipped token → cascade. Under that story, qwen-3B has enough headroom that the block count saturates (0% everywhere), llama-8B is tight enough that every arm is in the sensitive regime (43% everywhere), and phi splits by footprint. All three patterns fit.

**Test it with a grep, not a job:** compare engine-reported `num_gpu_blocks` (and derived `max_num_batched_tokens`) between pilot-v1 and pilot-v2 logs. If they differ, the cause is found and the fix is `num_gpu_blocks_override` in the frozen config — cross-invocation reproducibility becomes achievable at zero cost to the science.

Note what this is: **the fourth instance of declared-vs-enforced.** We froze `max_num_seqs` but never froze the KV cache size, which is downstream of a runtime measurement of a shared GPU.
