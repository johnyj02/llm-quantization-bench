## Metric: gate on `gsm8k_cot` permissive. Report all four. Strict is a diagnostic, not a block.

Anchor A's job is to detect a **calibration or serving error**, and those damage *competence*. Strict conflates competence with marker compliance — so gating on strict means a real compliance effect (which is a finding we want to measure) could spuriously fail a blocking gate and send you debugging a non-problem.

So **permissive is the correct gate metric**: it isolates the thing the gate is for, and at ~63% it has ample room.

**Reject (iii) as the gate.** Requiring strict to pass too makes the gate harder in the wrong direction: ±2.0 pp against a 13.5% value is a 15% relative band on a metric known to vary between checkpoints for reasons unrelated to calibration. That's false-failure risk on a blocking gate.

**Two-tier instead:** permissive within ±2.0 pp blocks. A strict discrepancy >2.0 pp is a **documented diagnostic requiring explanation**, not a block — and a large strict gap with a small permissive one is informative in its own right, since it points at chat-template or BOS handling rather than calibration.

`gsm8k_cot` over `gsm8k` is a disambiguation, not a substitution: both are GSM8K, and §9c already made `gsm8k_cot` primary.

## Do the free preflight before spending 1.2 h of GPU

**Read `quantize_config.json` / `config.json` from the three official checkpoints and assert `group_size`, `desc_act`/`actorder`, `bits`, symmetry, and quantization format match ours.** Zero generations, and it directly tests the most likely failure mode — a settings mismatch — which is what A9's "our calibration or serving config is wrong" actually means. Gate the generation run behind it.

## One framing point to state in the paper

The official Qwen checkpoints were calibrated on **their** data, not our `ultrachat_200k` 512×2048. So Anchor A does not test *reproduction* — it tests that our pipeline produces a checkpoint of **comparable quality**. ±2.0 pp is a quality-comparability band. Say so explicitly, or a reviewer reads the anchor as a reproduction claim it can't support.

## Item count: use the full 1319, and evaluate against the paired CI

The comparison is paired, so the SE on the difference is driven by item-level discordance, not by the ~63% marginals. At a plausible 10–20% correctness discordance between two different quantizations:

| n | SE on the difference | ±2.0 pp band |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | 1.4–2.0 pp | 1.0–1.4 SE |
| 1319 | 0.9–1.2 pp | 1.6–2.3 SE |

**At n=500 a perfect pipeline fails this gate roughly one time in six.** That is a badly calibrated blocking gate, and it runs once — 1319 costs ~2.6× of 1.2 h, call it 3 h and ~$9. Take it.

**And evaluate the gate against the interval, not the point alone:** report the point difference plus a paired bootstrap CI over items, pass on the point estimate, and pre-register that a CI straddling ±2.0 pp is recorded as **inconclusive, investigate** rather than a clean pass. Otherwise you'll be making a blocking decision on a number whose resolution you haven't stated — which is the exact criticism H7 levels at everyone else.
