## Q1 — Neither fork as stated. And you're about to commit the exact sin the paper diagnoses.

**The catch:** comparing *our* 1.6 pp (Qwen-3B, 500-item mixed probe) to *Kurtic's* 0.7 (Llama-405B) and *RedHat's* 0.1 (Llama-8B GSM8K) is a cross-model, cross-task, cross-protocol comparison — which is precisely what §0.1(b) says makes published deltas mutually incomparable. Build the headline on that comparison and a reviewer turns the paper's own thesis against it.

The internally-valid version of (a) needs **our own** 4-bit delta on the same models and probe. That lands with the reference runs. So don't write the comparative sentence yet.

What you can claim now is stronger than either fork, because it's about the *character* of the effect rather than its size:

> Reordering the evaluation set — a parameter no published protocol reports — shifts 4-bit accuracy by up to 1.6 points while leaving bf16 unchanged in all twelve configurations tested. The shift is deterministic given an ordering rather than sampling noise, so it cannot be averaged away; it is bidirectional, so no ordering is correct and it cannot be calibrated away; and it is attributable by intervention to the Marlin kernel path, which vLLM's batch-invariant mode does not cover.

Three properties — non-averageable, non-correctable, mechanistically pinned — and none depends on a magnitude comparison. Then one sentence added after the reference runs: *the ordering spread is X% of the bit-width effect we measure on the same items.*

Two wording notes: 1.6 pp is "up to 1.6 points," not "points" plural. And your churn observation belongs in the same paragraph — churn at 3–13× the aggregate means **the aggregate understates the instability**, which is the honest direction and links straight to the flips literature §7 requires us to engage.

## Q2 — (iii): spend the job, but the range is the wrong estimand.

Your statistical read is right and range-over-3 won't survive review. But the range is a bad estimator at any *n* — it's unstable and grows with sample size. Report the **distribution**: mean, SD, min, max across orderings, with SD as the inferential quantity and range as descriptive. "Accuracy has SD σ across random evaluation-set orderings" is defensible as a population claim; "the spread is 1.6 pp" never will be.

Scope: 4 arms (bf16, gptq-4bit, awq-4bit, bnb-nf4) at max_num_seqs=64 only, 500 items, **7 additional orderings** on top of your 3 → 10 total. 14,000 generations, ~35 min at your measured 24,800 gen/h. Keep 500 items rather than trading them for orderings — spread is paired across orderings so sampling variance largely cancels, but resolution doesn't: at 250 items one item is 0.4 pp and your whole effect is 8 items.

bf16 gets the same 10 orderings. Exactly-zero across ten is a much better control than across three.
