
- AWQ order-invariant → the source-level asymmetry *causes* the behavioural asymmetry. That is the strongest form H8 can take, because it's a confirmed prediction rather than a post-hoc explanation.
- AWQ also order-sensitive → the guard is insufficient. Different finding, also publishable, plus a substantive vLLM bug report.

Include bnb-NF4 and GPTQ-8bit too — the full arm ladder makes it a dose-response over kernel paths.

## Third: 65% is a text-level number. The paper needs the score-level one.

Your cascade reasoning is right — one flipped token diverges the trajectory, so 65% of *texts* differing is consistent with a tiny numerical perturbation. The corollary matters more than the argument: **report divergence at three levels — text, extracted answer, and correctness.**

65% text / 3% correctness and 65% text / 20% correctness are different papers, and you already have the generations to compute both. This is free and it's what Q2 actually hinges on.

## Fourth: R8's sweep criterion is dead. Reorient the sweep to the ordering axis.

The repeat-based sweep measured an axis with no variance — it printed "stable" for an arm that is 65% unstable. Sweeping `max_num_seqs` on repeats will keep printing "stable" at every level and tell you nothing.

**Replace: for each level in {8, 32, 64}, measure order-induced divergence across 3 orderings.** That's the decision-relevant comparison, and it may hand you 64 — which given Q4's throughput numbers is a 4× win on everything downstream.

## Q1 — (a), but freeze on the ordering axis, not by default

Agreed on (a)'s logic: ordering is fixed in our design, so our own deltas are reproducible, and order-sensitivity is a result rather than an obstacle. (b) would halt the study over a standard we don't actually need — order-invariance is required for *third parties to reproduce our strings*, not for *our comparisons to be valid*. Different standards; don't conflate them.

**But freezing 8 must be earned, not defaulted to.** You now have no evidence that 8 is better than 64 on the axis that varies — the repeat test can't distinguish them. If order-sensitivity is roughly flat across levels, 8 buys nothing and costs 4× throughput. Measure order-divergence at {8, 32, 64}, then freeze on that frontier and report it.

**Two hard requirements if you freeze under order-sensitivity:** the item order must be identical *across arms* (so ordering contributes zero **differential** noise between bf16 and GPTQ — this is precisely §4.6's differential-noise concern), and the order must be published as part of the artifact.

## Q2 — §9 binds to the repetition floor. Three numbers, three names.

You were right not to choose this after seeing data. The ruling:

1. **Repetition floor** — identical config, identical order, N repeats. Measured 0. **This is what §9's pre-commitment binds to**, because §9 protects against claiming effects that are pipeline noise *under the conditions we actually ran*, and we hold ordering fixed. That is not a dodge, provided ordering is common across arms.
2. **Reordering sensitivity** — the H8 headline. Not a floor on our deltas; a **reproducibility bound for third parties**. Report at all three levels (text / answer / correctness) per arm.
3. **Pipeline floor** (§4.5) — bf16 end-to-end double run, unchanged.

State it plainly in the paper: *our deltas are reproducible under our fixed, published item ordering; our generated strings are not reproducible under reordering for GPTQ; therefore any quantization study that does not fix and report item ordering carries an uncontrolled term.*

**That last clause is the sharpest thing this paper will say.** Nobody reports item ordering. It joins the §0.1(b) reporting-standards residue with a far harder edge than relative-vs-absolute units — and unlike that one, it's ours, measured, and mechanistically explained.

If correctness-level divergence turns out large (say >10 pp), come back to me — that would put pressure on reading (1) as sufficient, and I'd want to revisit before the freeze.

## Q3 — Confirmed, and the probe must be an ordering test

Your reasoning is exactly right: a repeat-based fallback probe would print "stable" for the same reason the sweep did and prove nothing. Trigger on ordering instability, and make the probe itself an ordering test.

Design it **paired**: GPTQ-4bit, the same 3 orderings, with and without `VLLM_DISABLED_KERNELS=MarlinLinearKernel,MacheteLinearKernel`. Order-divergence dropping to 0 with Marlin disabled pins the mechanism to a **named kernel** — that is H8's mechanism nailed down rather than inferred.

**And assert the selected linear kernel class in both conditions** (per the earlier ruling). Otherwise "disabled kernels" may silently have changed nothing, or the fallback may be a kernel you didn't expect, and the probe proves the opposite of what you'd conclude.
