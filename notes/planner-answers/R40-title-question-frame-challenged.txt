## But both your proposals have a factual problem, and it's worse than a framing weakness

**"Leaves bf16 unchanged" is false for one of the three factors you name.** R21 measured llama bf16 diverging 41/100 at the text level cross-job, with strict +1.11 pp and permissive +2.22 pp. So bf16 is exactly unchanged under **ordering** and under **within-job repetition** — and demonstrably *not* unchanged across job boundaries.

A title asserting three factors move 4-bit while bf16 holds still, when one of them moves bf16 too, is a claim a reviewer can falsify from our own Table. That's a worse outcome than any positioning error, and I let it through in R39.5 as much as you did.

**Second problem: "Nondeterminism" mischaracterises our own result.** We measured the repetition floor at exactly 0.000% and K=2 at 0.000% over 42,380 item-pairs. Generation is *deterministic given a configuration*; what varies is configuration we don't report. Calling that nondeterminism invites the reading "sampling noise," which we explicitly ruled out — and it discards the more surprising and more precise fact.

## Recommendation

Restrict the asymmetry claim to where it's exact, and let the subtitle carry the rest:

> **Item Ordering Moves 4-Bit Accuracy and Leaves bf16 Exactly Unchanged: Reproducibility Failures in Quantized Model Evaluation**

Why this over your two: the first clause is declarative, announces the empty cell, promises no quantity, is **true without qualification**, and foregrounds the one finding pinned by intervention (Marlin off → 0.000 on both 4-bit Marlin arms). "Exactly" is doing real work — it's the word that signals a measured zero rather than a hedge, and it's what makes the asymmetry arresting rather than incremental.

If you want the three factors named up front, they have to be accurate ones: **"Ordering, Job Boundaries, and Token Budgets"** — concrete, correct, and "job boundaries" is both more surprising and more precise than "nondeterminism." But then the bf16 clause has to go, and you lose the strongest thing in the title. I'd take the ordering-only version and put the other two factors in the abstract's second sentence.
