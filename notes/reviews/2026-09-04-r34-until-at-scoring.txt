## CRITICAL 1 — `generate()` ignores `until`, and the determinism probe still passes it

`src/quantartifact/generate.py:619` (parameter), `sm_determinism.py:392-393` (caller)

**Why it is wrong.** The diff replaced `stop=until` with `stop=None` inside `VLLMRunner.generate`, but left `until: list[str]` as a **required positional parameter**. It is now dead:

```
ARG002 Unused method argument: `until`
   --> src/quantartifact/generate.py:619:9
```

`run_cell` is fine — it stamps `until` on the record and the scorers apply it. But `run_cell` is not the only caller. `sm_determinism.py:389-393` calls the runner directly:

```python
groups.setdefault((item["max_gen_toks"], tuple(item["until"])), []).append(item)
for (max_toks, until), group in sorted(groups.items()):
    results = runner.generate([g["prompt"] for g in group], max_toks, list(until), ...)
```

**How it fails.** Three consequences, all silent:

1. **The probe's stored `outputs` become full-length generations.** They were truncated at `until` before; now they run to EOS or the 256-token cap. The decoding fingerprint now includes `until_applied: "scoring"`, so `cell_tag` changes and every cached probe cell correctly regenerates — no stale-cache bug. But what regenerates is different data.
2. **H8's text-divergence rates shift for a reason unrelated to batch invariance.** Divergence is measured on the raw output strings; longer strings give more opportunity to diverge. So the frontier table and `\hEightWorstSD` would move, and the movement would be attributed to batch composition when it is attributable to the terminator change. That is a wrong number in the section the H8 chapter leads with.
3. **`score_probe.py:121` extracts from untruncated text with no way to know.** It calls `score_generation(text, ...)` on `first["outputs"]`, and the probe stores **text only** — per CLAUDE.md §0 rule 4b, its per-pass keys are `ordering_seed`, `outputs` and `seconds`, with no `finish_reason`, no `n_gen_tokens` and therefore no `until_applied`. So `scored_text_for` cannot be applied to a probe pass even in principle. Post-R34 probe accuracy numbers would credit answers appearing after a terminator, which is precisely the "mirror image of the bug R34 fixed" your own `label_compliance` comment warns against.

This is the charter's named pattern — a parameter accepted and then ignored — with a live caller that reads as configured and is not.

**Confirmed by:** execution (`ruff --select ARG`), plus reading `sm_determinism.py:389-393` and `score_probe.py:105-128`.

**Fix, and I would not submit the smoke without it.** Decide the probe's regime explicitly rather than by omission:

- If the probe should keep generation-time truncation (defensible: it measures text divergence, and comparability with the already-published H8 numbers has value), then `generate()` needs to take the regime as an argument rather than dropping `stop` unconditionally — for example `stop=until if until_at_generation else None` — and `sm_determinism` passes `True`.
- If the probe should move to the scoring regime, then its stored passes must carry `until` and `until_applied`, and `score_probe` must route through `scored_text_for`. That also means the previously measured H8 divergence rates are superseded, which is a planner matter, not a code one.

Either way, **remove the dead parameter or make it live**. Leaving a required argument that does nothing is how `add_bos_token` and `async_scheduling` happened. And add `ARG` to the ruff selection: it caught this in one command.

## Q1 — the ordering is correct, and here is the proof

Under the old regime, vLLM halts at whichever comes first in token order: a `stop` match, EOS, or `max_tokens`. So for any generation:

| full text contains a terminator? | old regime outcome | new regime under your logic |
|---|---|---|
| yes, at token k | halts at k, `finish_reason="stop"`, `stop_reason=<str>` → TRUNC_STOPSEQ (if uncommitted) | terminator found → TRUNC_STOPSEQ (if uncommitted) ✓ |
| no, and the run hit the cap | `finish_reason="length"` → TRUNC_BUDGET | no terminator, `finish_reason=="length"` → TRUNC_BUDGET ✓ |
| no, and the run hit EOS | `finish_reason="stop"`, `stop_reason` None/EOS → COMPLETE | no terminator, not length → COMPLETE ✓ |

The key step is the one your docstring asserts: **a terminator present anywhere in the full text necessarily occurred at a token position at or before where generation actually ended**, so the old engine would have cut there and could never have reached the cap. That makes terminator-presence strictly dominant over `finish_reason == "length"`, which is exactly your ordering.

The remaining case worth checking is the one that looks like a counterexample and is not: a generation whose terminator would have appeared *beyond* the cap. Then the new text has no terminator and hits the cap → TRUNC_BUDGET; and the old regime also saw no terminator within the cap → TRUNC_BUDGET. They agree. **I could not construct a case where your ordering is wrong.**

One thing worth adding to the docstring: the classification is not merely "reasonable under the new regime", it is **provably identical to what the old regime would have produced**, which is a stronger claim and the one §4's differential test needs.

## Q4 — clean, and the reason is that `until` is on the record

`apply_until` is idempotent by construction: truncating at the first terminator leaves text containing no terminator, so a second pass finds nothing. Your test pins it.

Double truncation with a *different* list is closed, and it is closed by schema rather than by discipline: `scored_text_for` uses `record["until"]`, `label_compliance` receives `record.get("until")`, and both therefore use the list that generation actually used — which for BBH is the level-specific set, since `until` and `template_level` are both stamped. The wrong-template-level scenario you worried about needs a record with no `until`, and a scoring-regime record always has one (schema 1.4.0 implies 1.2.0).

One residual worth a comment rather than a fix: `_resolve_terminators` falls back to `HARNESS_UNTIL[task]`, the **union across both template levels**, when `until is None`. Under the scoring regime that union would drive `apply_until`, so a hypothetical `until`-less scoring record would be truncated at whichever level's terminator appeared first — possibly the wrong level's. Unreachable today; worth an assertion that `until_applied == "scoring"` implies `until is not None`, since that is a schema invariant you can state in one line.

## Q5 — three things, and two of them are yours to decide

**(a) The determinism probe.** CRITICAL 1. Your instinct to look there was right.

**(b) `n_gen_tokens` changes meaning across the regime boundary, and is aggregated under one key.** `score_pilot.py:259-261` computes `n_gen_tokens_mean` and `n_gen_tokens_median` from `record["n_gen_tokens"]` with no regime branch. Pre-R34 records report the kept prefix; post-R34 report the full generation including everything after the terminator. Same field, same report key, incomparable quantities — so a `pilot-v2` median printed beside a `main-v1` median would be comparing prefix length against full length.

This matters more than a display inconsistency because `records.py:12` stakes a paper claim on the field: *"`n_gen_tokens` — same, and it settles the arXiv:2606.25519 vs arXiv:2504.04823 contradiction about whether quantization lengthens CoT."* The full-generation measure is arguably the *better* instrument for that question, since it no longer truncates at an arbitrary terminator — but it is not the same instrument, and nothing records which one produced a given median. At minimum, key the aggregate on the regime and refuse to pool. Whether the CoT-length claim should now be made on the full or the truncated length is a planner question, and it is worth asking before `main-v1` rather than after, because the answer might be "record both" and the truncated length is derivable only if the scorer stores it.

**(c) `peek_generations.py:117` — rule 4b's primary heuristic inverts.** The script tells the reader: *"Is `n_gen_tokens` far below `max_gen_toks`? On a CoT task that is suspicious."* Under the old regime a stop sequence firing early produced exactly that signature, and it is how this very defect was found. Post-R34, a terminator no longer shortens `n_gen_tokens`, so the check goes quiet on the failure mode it exists to catch. The replacement signal is available and better: a large gap between `len(generation_text)` and the length after `apply_until`. Since you are about to run rule 4b on the smoke, updating the printed guidance is worth doing first, or the check will read as clean for the wrong reason.
